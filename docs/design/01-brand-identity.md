# 01 — Brand Identity

## Positioning

KUNDE is an everyday digital companion that helps people find, compare and manage real local services in one place. It should feel helpful and dependable, not like a financial institution or an AI novelty. The core promise follows PRD 1.0: find what is needed, compare options, act with control, and manage the result.

## Personality and values

| Value | Observable design behavior |
|---|---|
| Helpful | Put the next useful action near the information it depends on; offer alternatives when a service is unavailable. |
| Trustworthy | Show price basis, status freshness, cancellation terms and confirmed vs pending state clearly. |
| Local-first | Support Kazakh and Russian naturally; respect local address, date, currency and service-area conventions. |
| Calm | Use a restrained palette, whitespace and predictable navigation; reserve strong color for action/status. |
| User-controlled | Confirm payment and consequential AI actions; make permissions and data sharing understandable. |
| Inclusive | Use readable type, visible labels, touch targets, screen-reader semantics and manual alternatives. |

## Voice and naming

Name is always **KUNDE** in brand lockups; product UI can use “KUNDE AI” for the assistant as specified in PRD. Do not abbreviate to K, alter spelling or imply a legal entity name. Use sentence case in product headings. Avoid slang, exaggerated claims, urgency pressure and unexplained AI language. Use “KUNDE” as a noun only when it improves clarity; otherwise prefer “we” sparingly and name the action.

The tagline is exactly **Everyday, made easier.** Use on launch/brand surfaces and marketing materials where space allows; do not force it into transactional screens. Do not translate the English master lockup until translations are reviewed.

## Visual directions considered

| Direction | Character | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Calm Teal + Warm Mineral (recommended) | Evergreen/teal, warm off-white, graphite, small citrus accent | Clear trust cue, warm and distinctive while flexible across categories | Green is common in service apps; needs own illustration and typography details |
| B. Urban Ink + Apricot | Deep ink blue, pale stone, apricot | Premium, strong text contrast, works for mobility/experiences | Can feel formal/corporate and less welcoming for daily household use |
| C. Bright Cobalt + Lime | Cobalt, cool gray, electric lime | High energy, high visibility and modern digital feel | More tiring for daily utility and risks status/action confusion |

**Recommendation:** prototype Direction A. It supports long daily sessions, the KZ market’s bilingual text density, and a consistent base for consumer/operations tools. The palette is a hypothesis; validate with users and contrast tests. Do not copy recognizable competitor layouts or logos.

## Logo and mark brief (not final artwork)

Explore a custom wordmark with a sturdy geometric sans, open counters and distinctive rhythm. Explore a simple “day / everyday cycle” symbol using a rounded arc or horizon motif; it must remain legible at 16 px and work in one color. Avoid literal sun clichés, map pins, shopping bags and AI sparkles as the only brand mark. Keep “AI” as a feature label, not in the master wordmark.

Proposed lockups: horizontal wordmark + optional mark; compact app icon with mark only; monochrome version. Clear space ≥ one mark-stroke width or a measured internal unit established during logo design. Minimum sizes and exclusion zone must be set after vector concept testing. Never stretch, rotate, add shadows, recolor outside approved palette or place on low-contrast photography. Final logo, trademark and app-store icon require separate review/approval.

## Iconography and visual language

Use a consistent rounded-line icon family with 2 px-equivalent stroke at 24 px, rounded joins and clear filled selected states. Prefer familiar object/action metaphors, supported by labels. Category illustrations may use simple geometric scenes and local real photography with consent/license. Never use emoji as production category iconography. Photography should show authentic, inclusive urban life; avoid identifiable user/provider photos without permission.

## Across the ecosystem

Consumer can be expressive and spacious; Partner prioritizes catalog and task speed; Provider prioritizes glanceable one-handed actions outdoors; Admin prioritizes dense but scannable information. All share color roles, typography, radii, icons, form behavior, status semantics and brand mark. Their information hierarchy differs by role and permission. Any role must see only authorized data.
